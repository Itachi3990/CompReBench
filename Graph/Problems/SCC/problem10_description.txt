Sociologists are studying how rumors and unverified information spread through a massive corporate office. They have mapped out a directed network of "who gossips to whom." Employee A might tell secrets to Employee B, but B might not trust A enough to reciprocate. 

The researchers want to find the core "rumor mills" within the company to understand how misinformation survives debunking efforts. 

They need to mathematically partition the social graph to identify tightly knit, maximal cliques of employees. Within these specific groups, a rumor started by any single person will eventually circulate through complex chains of workplace gossip to reach every other person in that group, creating a self-sustaining echo chamber of office politics.
