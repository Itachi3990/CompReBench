In the distant future, the star system of Veridian Prime is facing an apocalyptic crisis. The system's central star has destabilized and is projected to go supernova within the month, threatening the lives of twelve billion citizens. The Galactic Federation has initiated a massive, unprecedented evacuation protocol, aiming to transport the entire population to a designated 'Sanctuary System' located in a neighboring sector. However, interstellar travel of this magnitude cannot rely on standard warp drives; it requires the use of an ancient, pre-existing network of stable wormholes, subspace transit relays, and deep-space waystations left behind by an older civilization. 

This network is highly complex and non-linear. The evacuation fleet, assembling at the primary staging ground over Veridian Prime, must navigate through various intermediate star systems, splitting up and recombining to utilize all available routes to the Sanctuary System. The critical bottleneck is the infrastructure itself. Each wormhole and subspace relay is incredibly fragile and can only handle a specific maximum capacity of transit mass per hour. 

For instance, the 'Tannhauser Gate' wormhole might allow 500 massive refugee cruisers to pass through every hour, but it connects to a waystation that only has three departing relays, each with a capacity of 100 cruisers per hour. Pushing more ships through a wormhole than its maximum rated capacity would cause a catastrophic gravitational collapse, destroying the ships and severing the route permanently. 

The Grand Admiral of the evacuation fleet is staring at a holographic map of this transit network. Time is running out. They cannot simply send all ships down the single widest path; doing so would leave billions behind. They must utilize the entire network—every branching path, every parallel relay, every convoluted detour—to maximize the sheer volume of lives saved. 

The Admiral requires the fleet's quantum supercomputers to solve a monumental optimization problem. They must process the entire topology of the transit network, analyzing the strict 'ships-per-hour' capacity of every single wormhole and relay between Veridian Prime and the Sanctuary System. The ultimate objective is to calculate the absolute maximum theoretical throughput of the entire network. The computer must determine the exact maximum number of refugee cruisers that can successfully arrive at the Sanctuary System every single hour if the fleet perfectly utilizes all available parallel pathways simultaneously. 

By calculating this maximum flow limit, the Grand Admiral will know exactly how many billions of citizens can be saved before the star explodes, and they can schedule the launch of the refugee ships to perfectly saturate the network without ever exceeding the fatal capacity of any single wormhole. The emotional toll on the planners is immense. Every fraction of a capacity unit optimized means thousands of families reaching safety. The algorithm must also account for the 'conservation of ships'—no cruisers can be created or destroyed at the intermediate waystations; the number of ships entering a junction must exactly equal the number leaving, unless it is the final Sanctuary. If the computer reveals that the absolute maximum throughput is insufficient to save the entire population in the given timeframe, the Admiral will be forced to make unthinkable triage decisions. Therefore, the mathematical proof of the maximum flow is the most critical calculation in the history of the Federation.
