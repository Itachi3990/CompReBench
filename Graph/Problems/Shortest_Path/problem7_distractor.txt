The United Earth Directorate is attempting to establish the first stable quantum communication link with the newly founded Mars colony, 'Ares Prime.' The communication network relies on a fragile web of quantum entanglement relays positioned on various satellites, orbital platforms, and lunar bases between the two planets. During the initial systems integration phase, the theoretical physicists demanded an "all pairs shortest path" calculation. They wanted the central supercomputer to pre-compute a massive matrix containing the absolute lowest latency connection between every single relay station and every other relay station in the entire solar system simultaneously. They assumed that having a pre-calculated, universally complete routing table was the only way to ensure network stability. 

Simultaneously, the quantum mechanics team attempted to impose a "topological sort of the quantum states." They were obsessing over the precise temperatures of the qubits in Kelvin and the exact physical dimensions of the relay processors in nanometers, trying to force a strict chronological sequence of quantum superposition collapses across the network. They believed that organizing the data transmission chronologically was the key to preventing signal degradation.

However, the lead network engineer immediately halted both of these massive computational endeavors. While it might seem like pre-computing every possible route across the entire solar system, or mathematically sequencing the chronological order of quantum states is the most robust way to build a communication grid, these approaches waste incredibly precious processing power on irrelevant data. 

The true problem is focused and highly specific. The Directorate does not need to communicate between a random lunar mining platform and a Venusian atmospheric probe. They only care about establishing a single, secure line between the Earth Command Center and the Ares Prime command bunker. 

The network is a complex web of potential relay-to-relay connections. Each jump between two specific relays incurs a known "decoherence penalty"—a measure of how much signal integrity is lost during the transfer, heavily dependent on localized solar radiation and gravitational interference. The goal is not to find a route with the fewest number of jumps, but the route that preserves the most signal integrity. 

Therefore, the lead engineer is NOT trying to calculate an all pairs shortest path matrix for the entire solar system. They are NOT trying to chronologically order the quantum states, and they are NOT trying to find the maximum possible volume of data throughput. 

Instead, they need an algorithm to analyze the map of the entanglement relays and the specific decoherence penalties associated with every potential connection. The sole objective is to compute the single optimal sequence of relay jumps that begins at Earth Command and ends exactly at Ares Prime. Crucially, the algorithm must ensure that the total, cumulative decoherence penalty accumulated along that specific path is the absolute mathematical minimum, guaranteeing that the critical message reaches the Mars colony intact.
