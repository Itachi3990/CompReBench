The interstellar mining frigate 'Aegis' has received a faint distress signal from a survey shuttle trapped deep within the treacherous 'Kaldor's Maw' asteroid field. The field is a chaotic maelstrom of constantly shifting debris, volatile gas pockets, and intense magnetic anomalies. During the initial frantic response, the frigate's engineering deck submitted a proposal to calculate the "minimum spanning tree of the asteroids." They wanted to deploy a network of tether cables connecting every single stable asteroid in the field using the absolute minimum total length of high-tensile wire, believing that establishing a rigid, interconnected infrastructure was the safest way to stabilize the region before launching a rescue. 

Simultaneously, the tactical division was obsessed with calculating the "maximum flow of starships." They spent hours analyzing the precise mineral composition of the asteroids and measuring their volume in exact cubic kilometers, attempting to determine the maximum sheer number of rescue shuttles they could simultaneously push through the widest gaps in the debris field without causing a catastrophic collision. They treated the asteroid field like a massive pipeline that needed to be flooded with rescue craft.

However, the seasoned Captain of the Aegis immediately rejected both proposals. While it might seem like establishing a secure, minimally connected network of tether cables across the entire field, or launching the maximum possible armada of rescue ships is the most robust response to an emergency, these strategies completely ignore the immediate reality of the trapped shuttle. The survey shuttle is leaking oxygen; they do not have the time to build infrastructure, nor do they have the fuel to launch an armada. 

The true problem is singular and immediate. The Aegis has only one heavily armored rescue pod capable of surviving the magnetic anomalies. The asteroid field is a complex web of narrow, navigable corridors between the shifting debris. Each corridor requires a very specific, estimated expenditure of the pod's limited thruster fuel to navigate safely, heavily dependent on the localized gravity wells and gas pockets. 

Therefore, the Captain is NOT trying to connect all the asteroids with a minimum spanning tree. They are NOT trying to calculate the maximum flow of ships through the field, and they are NOT trying to chronologically order the destruction of the asteroids. 

Instead, the navigation AI must analyze the entire map of the asteroid field and the specific fuel costs associated with traversing each potential corridor. The sole objective is to compute the absolute most fuel-efficient sequence of corridors to navigate. The AI must calculate the exact route that begins at the Aegis's airlock and ends directly at the survey shuttle's distress beacon, ensuring that the total, cumulative expenditure of thruster fuel along that specific route is the absolute mathematical minimum. They just need the single cheapest path to the target before the oxygen runs out.
