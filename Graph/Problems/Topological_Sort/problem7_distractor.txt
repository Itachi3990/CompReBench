The Genesis Engine is a planetary-scale terraforming machine buried deep within the crust of the dead world, Tartarus. The machine consists of over five thousand gargantuan sub-systems, ranging from atmospheric scrubbers to tectonic stabilizers. The engineering corps is currently preparing for the engine's maiden ignition sequence. During the preliminary briefings, the safety inspectors became incredibly anxious about power surges. They insisted on running diagnostics dedicated to "detecting cycles in the power grid," terrified that a feedback loop of raw plasma would incinerate the control center. They spent weeks trying to prove that the electrical schematic was completely free of any closed loops.

Simultaneously, the thermal dynamics team was focused on physical infrastructure. They wanted to build a "minimum spanning tree of the cooling pipes." They obsessed over the precise temperature of the magma core in Kelvin and the physical dimensions of the engine in kilometers, attempting to route the liquid nitrogen coolant through the absolute minimum total length of piping to conserve material. They treated the entire ignition sequence as a plumbing optimization problem. 

However, the Chief Ignition Officer quickly realized that both of these approaches were focusing on the wrong phase of the operation. While it might seem like ensuring the power grid is cycle-free, or finding the absolute shortest total distance to route coolant is the primary goal, those are structural issues that were resolved during construction. The engine is built. The problem now is how to turn it on.

The ignition of the Genesis Engine is an incredibly delicate, cascading chain reaction. The sub-systems cannot simply be turned on all at once. The atmospheric scrubbers cannot be powered until the external vents are opened. The vents cannot be opened until the tectonic stabilizers have equalized the pressure. The stabilizers cannot fire until the central plasma core is pre-heated. These operational dependencies are rigid and uncompromising. If a single sub-system is activated before its prerequisites are fully online, the resulting imbalance will trigger a catastrophic planetary implosion.

Therefore, the Chief Ignition Officer is NOT trying to detect cycles in the power grid. They are NOT trying to build a minimum spanning tree of the cooling pipes, and they are NOT trying to perfectly pair specific engineers with specific control consoles. 

Instead, they need an algorithm to process the massive database of the five thousand sub-systems and their strict, one-way operational dependencies. The algorithm must produce a single, definitive master boot sequence. This sequence must be a perfectly linear, chronological checklist. It must mathematically guarantee that for every single sub-system listed, absolutely all of its required prerequisite systems appear earlier in the boot sequence. Only by following this strictly sorted, chronological roadmap can the engineering corps safely awaken the Genesis Engine and breathe life into the dead world.
