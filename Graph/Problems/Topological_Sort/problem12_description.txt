The Zenith Corporation has purchased the rights to colonize and terraform Kepler-186f, a barren, rocky exoplanet located 500 light-years from Earth. The planet currently possesses a toxic, thin atmosphere, a frozen surface, and absolutely zero biological life. Transforming this desolate rock into a lush, Earth-like paradise is a monumental ecological engineering endeavor expected to take over three centuries. The project is broken down into hundreds of macro-ecological phases, chemical interventions, and biological seeding events. 

Because terraforming is a delicate, cascading process, the order in which these interventions occur is paramount. Every step is a strict prerequisite for the next, forming a massive, planetary-scale dependency graph. If the corporation attempts to introduce complex life too early, the organisms will suffocate and die, wasting billions of credits and decades of progress. 

The process must begin with atmospheric engineering. 'Orbital Mirror Deployment' must be completed first to reflect sunlight and warm the planet. Only after the planet reaches a specific temperature threshold can 'Subterranean Ice Melting' begin. The melting ice will create oceans, which is a strict physical prerequisite for 'Cyanobacteria Seeding.' The cyanobacteria will slowly convert the toxic atmosphere into oxygen. 

The biological dependencies are even more rigid. 'Lichen and Moss Introduction' requires the oxygen levels to reach 5%, and they are necessary to begin breaking down the rocky surface into usable soil. 'Basic Flora Seeding' (grasses and simple shrubs) cannot occur until the soil depth reaches ten centimeters. Moving up the food chain, 'Herbivore Introduction' is strictly dependent on the establishment of a self-sustaining flora population to prevent the animals from starving. Finally, 'Apex Predator Introduction' can only happen once the herbivore population is large enough to support them without collapsing the ecosystem. 

The Zenith Corporation's Terraforming Director oversees a massive AI simulation of the planet. They have a database of every single ecological phase and its strict environmental prerequisites. They need to generate a master, multi-generational timeline for the robotic seeding ships in orbit. 

The AI must process the vast web of chemical, geological, and biological dependencies and output a strict, linear chronological plan. This plan will dictate the exact order of operations for the next three hundred years. It must guarantee that no ecological phase is ever initiated before its required environmental and biological foundations have been firmly established. By following this sorted roadmap, the corporation ensures that each new layer of the ecosystem is built upon a stable, viable foundation, slowly coaxing a dead world into a vibrant biosphere.

This chronological blueprint must be passed down through generations of planetary engineers. If a future director grows impatient and attempts to seed hardwood forests before the nitrogen-fixing bacteria have fully saturated the soil, the forests will fail, setting the project back decades. The strict sequential ordering of events is the only thing standing between a successful colonization effort and a catastrophic ecological collapse. The algorithm must untangle the complex web of life, recognizing that while many species depend on multiple others, the fundamental building blocks of an ecosystem must be laid down one by one. The resulting linear sequence transforms the chaotic complexity of nature into a methodical, step-by-step engineering manual for creating a world from scratch.
