Deep beneath the frozen crust of Europa, a swarm of autonomous aquatic drones, designated the "Leviathan Series," has been deployed into a newly discovered, pitch-black subterranean ocean. The ocean floor is not flat; it is a chaotic, three-dimensional labyrinth of hydrothermal vents, narrow crevices, and massive, interconnected cave systems. The primary mission of the Leviathan swarm is absolute cartographic exhaustion. 

When the mission was first proposed, orbital scientists engaged in fierce debates about the theoretical "minimum spanning tree of the seafloor," arguing that the drones should prioritize connecting all major thermal vents using the shortest possible total distance of travel paths to conserve battery power. Others spent months calculating the precise pressure ratings in atmospheres for every known trench and plotting the exact depth coordinates in meters, insisting that a strict "topological ordering of the trenches" was necessary to prevent the drones from imploding. They believed that organizing the exploration strictly by depth was the only logical approach.

However, once the drones breached the ice crust, the mission parameters had to be completely overhauled. The cave systems are filled with confusing, winding tunnels that often loop back on themselves or intersect with previously discovered chambers. While it might seem like the primary directive should be finding the single most efficient, cheapest path back to the insertion point, or that minimizing the overall battery expenditure of the swarm is the most critical metric, the true danger lies in the geometry of the caves themselves.

If a Leviathan drone simply follows a tunnel without a strict, systemic memory, it will eventually wander in a circle. It will re-scan the exact same hydrothermal vent, follow the exact same adjoining tunnel, and end up right back where it started, trapped in a continuous loop until its power core dies, leaving vast sectors of the underground ocean completely unexplored.

The expedition commanders are NOT looking to find the shortest path out of the caves. They are NOT trying to connect the thermal vents with the minimum total travel distance, and they are NOT attempting to order the caverns chronologically or by depth. 

Instead, the drone swarm operates on a unified, hive-mind exploration protocol focused solely on discovery. Starting from the central insertion shaft, the swarm must probe the darkness, identifying every single adjoining corridor. Before physically traveling down a new passage, the drone transmits the unique sonar signature of the entrance to the central command hub. The hub checks this signature against a continuously updating registry of "Charted Locations." 

If the cavern is new, it is marked as charted, and its connecting tunnels are queued up for further exploration. If the cavern has already been logged by another drone, the current drone is immediately instructed to turn around and explore a different, uncharted path. By relentlessly pushing this boundary outward and meticulously recording where they have already been, the swarm ensures that every single physically reachable chamber in the alien ocean is eventually discovered and mapped, without ever getting trapped endlessly re-processing the same circular tunnels.
