At the global headquarters of OmniCorp, a devastating cybersecurity crisis is unfolding. A sophisticated, self-replicating malware worm known as 'Cerberus' has breached the outer firewall. Cerberus is designed to aggressively propagate through the corporation's massive internal network (the Intranet), seeking out highly classified R&D databases to encrypt and hold for ransom. The company's Intranet is a sprawling, wildly complex web of interconnected subnets, legacy servers, employee workstations, remote VPN nodes, and specialized laboratory equipment. 

The cybersecurity Incident Response (IR) team has managed to identify the exact entry point: a compromised email server in the European branch office. However, Cerberus moves incredibly fast, hopping from the email server to any machine it shares a local network connection with, and from those machines to further subnets. The IR team cannot simply shut down the entire global network without costing the company billions in lost revenue. They must surgically track the worm's exact path of propagation and quarantine only the affected sectors.

To achieve this, the IR team unleashes their proprietary 'Hunter-Seeker' diagnostic AI. The AI's mandate is to trace the infection outwards from the initial compromised email server. The AI must systematically query the email server to determine which other machines it has communicated with in the last ten minutes. For every connected machine discovered, the AI must log into it, scan it for the Cerberus signature, and then identify what further machines that computer has subsequently contacted.

The primary technical challenge for the Hunter-Seeker AI is the sheer interconnectedness of the corporate network. An infected workstation in marketing might connect to a shared printer, which is also connected to a clean workstation in accounting, which is connected back to the main server. The network is full of these massive, overlapping digital loops and redundant pathways. 

If the AI were to blindly follow every connection without a tracking mechanism, it would become trapped in an endless cycle. It would scan the marketing computer, follow the link to the printer, follow the link to the accounting computer, follow the link back to the main server, and then follow the link right back to marketing—creating an infinite loop of redundant scanning that would crash the diagnostic system while Cerberus continued to spread unchecked.

Therefore, the core of the AI's logic is an unyielding, high-speed memory ledger. Before scanning any newly discovered IP address, the AI checks the address against its 'Processed List'. If the machine is already on the list—meaning it has already been scanned and either quarantined or cleared—the AI immediately drops the connection and moves on to the next unchecked pathway. 

This tracking protocol forces the AI to fan out from the source of the infection in a systematic, methodical wave. It explores the digital topology of the Intranet, discovering all reachable nodes stemming from the initial breach. By rigorously marking every visited server and endpoint, the AI guarantees that it will eventually identify every single machine exposed to the worm, mapping the total extent of the infection without ever wasting precious seconds rescanning a system twice. Only through this exhaustive, non-repeating exploration can the IR team hope to isolate the Cerberus worm before it reaches the core financial databases.
