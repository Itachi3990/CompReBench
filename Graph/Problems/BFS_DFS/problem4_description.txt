On the desert planet of Aethelgard, a team of xenobiologists and archaeologists has unearthed the entrance to a massive, subterranean labyrinth built by a long-extinct alien civilization known as the Precursors. The entrance leads into a staggering network of pitch-black catacombs, intersecting tunnels, vertical shafts, and vast cavernous chambers. Ground-penetrating radar indicates the complex extends for hundreds of miles in every direction, but the layout is chaotic, non-euclidean, and completely unmapped. 

The expedition's primary objective is to locate a rumored 'Central Archive'—a specific chamber containing the culmination of Precursor scientific knowledge. However, sending human explorers into the labyrinth is deemed a suicide mission due to the high risk of getting permanently lost. Instead, the team deploys a swarm of autonomous, dog-sized mapping rovers called 'Scarab Drones.'

The drones are injected into the primary entrance shaft. Their programming dictates a rigid, systematic exploration protocol. Starting from their current location, the drones must probe the darkness, identifying every single adjoining corridor or doorway. They will physically travel down a tunnel, scan the new chamber they arrive in, and then look for further exits. This process must be repeated relentlessly, pushing deeper and deeper into the alien ruins.

However, the greatest danger to the mission is not traps or indigenous wildlife; it is the labyrinth's architecture itself. The ruins are filled with confusing circular passageways, dead ends, and chambers with multiple entrances that lead back to previously explored areas. If the drones simply wandered randomly or followed paths without a strict memory system, they would inevitably end up flying in circles, their batteries dying long before they could map even a fraction of the complex.

To prevent this catastrophic failure, the Scarab swarm's central hive-mind computer maintains a massive, real-time digital blueprint. As a drone enters a new chamber, it immediately transmits the unique topological signature of that room back to the central computer. The algorithm checks this signature against its massive database of 'Visited Locations.' 

If the room is new, the computer logs it as 'visited' and adds all of its connecting tunnels to a priority queue for future exploration. If the room has already been logged, the drone is immediately instructed to turn around and explore a different, unvisited path. This rigorous, methodical process ensures that the swarm's frontier of exploration is constantly expanding outward, pushing deeper into the unknown without ever wasting processing power or battery life on a room that has already been charted. 

The expedition scientists watch the map bloom on their monitors. Sometimes, a drone will spend hours navigating a complex, winding series of shafts, only to break through a wall and find itself back in the main entrance hall. The mapping algorithm immediately prunes that path, recognizing the loop, and redirects the drone. The true test of the algorithm is its exhaustiveness. The scientists are terrified that they might miss a hidden, narrow crevice leading to the Archive simply because the drones failed to search a specific corner. Therefore, the exploration protocol guarantees that if a chamber physically connects to the entrance by any sequence of paths, no matter how convoluted or hidden, it will eventually be discovered and processed. The fate of humanity's technological leap forward relies entirely on this flawless, non-repeating traversal of the ancient dark.
