The Emerald Expanse, a massive national park covering millions of acres, is currently engulfed in the largest wildfire in recorded history. The fire is advancing rapidly toward the densely populated city of Oakhaven. In the initial chaotic response, the regional fire marshals panicked and ordered a "maximum flow of water" protocol. They mobilized every available tanker truck and aerial water bomber, desperately trying to calculate the absolute maximum volume of water in gallons that could be dumped onto the front lines per hour. They treated the crisis as a sheer volume contest, hoping they could simply drown the fire by pushing the maximum possible throughput of retardant onto the burning canopy. 

Simultaneously, a team of environmental scientists submitted a frantic proposal to establish a "minimum spanning tree of the firebreak." They wanted to connect all the major ecological preservation zones in the park with a network of thin, precisely cut fire lines, using the absolute minimum total miles of cleared brush. They were obsessing over saving specific species of rare pine trees and tracking the exact acreage of lost habitat, believing that keeping the firebreak network as sparse as possible would preserve the ecosystem. 

However, the grizzled Incident Commander arriving on the scene immediately grounded the water bombers and halted the environmentalists. While it might seem like the paramount goal during a wildfire is to dump the maximum possible volume of water onto the flames, or to preserve the environment by building the thinnest, most minimal network of firebreaks, these strategies will not save Oakhaven. The fire is too hot, and the wind is too strong. Water evaporates before it hits the ground, and sparse firebreaks will be easily jumped by flying embers. 

The true challenge is brutal, calculated isolation. The forest is a complex topographical web of ridges, valleys, dry riverbeds, and dense thickets. The Incident Commander commands a fleet of heavy bulldozers, but bulldozing a wide, effective firebreak through different types of terrain takes vastly different amounts of time and fuel. 

Therefore, the Incident Commander is NOT trying to maximize the flow of water or retardant. They are NOT trying to connect the preservation zones with a minimum spanning tree, and they are NOT trying to find the shortest path for the fire trucks to reach the front lines. 

Instead, they need an algorithm to analyze the entire topographical map of the forest between the advancing fire front and the city of Oakhaven. The algorithm must consider the specific time and effort required to bulldoze a firebreak across every individual ridge and valley. The objective is to identify the absolute perfect choke point in the terrain. The algorithm must calculate the specific combination of geographical features that, if completely bulldozed and cleared of fuel, will completely sever the continuous canopy and isolate the fire from the city. Crucially, this specific set of cleared lines must represent the absolute minimum total bulldozing effort necessary to achieve complete isolation before the wind shifts. They need to find the weakest link in the forest's fuel connectivity to make their stand.
