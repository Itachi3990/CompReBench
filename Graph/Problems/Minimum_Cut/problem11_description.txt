The nation of Oakhaven is under a massive, coordinated cyber-warfare attack from an unknown state actor. The attack is a highly sophisticated, multi-pronged intrusion originating from a massive offshore botnet. The ultimate target of the attack is Oakhaven's Central Defense Mainframe, which controls the nation's automated missile defense systems. If the botnet manages to push its malicious payload through the national network and corrupt the mainframe, the country will be left entirely defenseless. 

Oakhaven's national Intranet is a sprawling, wildly complex beast. It is a dense web of civilian ISPs, military-grade fiber-optic trunks, satellite uplinks, regional data centers, and localized firewall clusters. Data flowing from the external botnet to the defense mainframe does not take a single path; it filters through thousands of overlapping, redundant connections. Each of these connections—whether it's a physical fiber cable under the ocean or a logical port on a massive enterprise router—has a specific maximum data capacity (bandwidth). 

The National Cyber Defense Command (NCDC) is in full panic mode. They cannot simply pull the plug on the defense mainframe, as taking it offline manually would trigger the exact vulnerability they are trying to prevent. They also cannot disconnect the entire country from the global internet without causing an economic collapse that would rival a physical bombing campaign. 

The NCDC's only option is a surgical 'defensive severing.' They must intentionally destroy or permanently shut down a specific set of network connections within their own infrastructure. The goal is to create an impenetrable digital moat—to completely sever all possible pathways of data flowing from the compromised offshore botnet to the defense mainframe. 

However, every connection they sever causes collateral damage. Shutting down a major fiber trunk might cut off hospital communications in a major city; disabling a satellite uplink might blind regional air traffic control. The 'cost' of severing a connection is directly proportional to its bandwidth capacity. The NCDC's supercomputers must solve a horrifying optimization problem. 

They must mathematically analyze the entire topological graph of the national network. They need to find the absolute 'minimum cut.' This is the specific combination of network links that, if simultaneously severed, will completely disconnect the botnet from the mainframe, while ensuring that the total combined bandwidth of the severed links is the absolute mathematical minimum possible. 

Finding this minimum cut identifies the theoretical bottleneck of the attack. By sacrificing this specific, minimal set of connections, the NCDC can completely isolate the defense mainframe and stop the cyber-attack dead in its tracks, while preserving the maximum possible amount of connectivity and economic functionality for the rest of the nation. The algorithm must be flawless; if they miss a single logical pathway, the payload will slip through, and if they cut too much, they will plunge their own country into a digital dark age. The tension in the command center is palpable. As the algorithm crunches the billions of nodes and edges representing the nation's digital lifeblood, commanders argue over the ethical implications of the impending blackout. When the supercomputer finally outputs the exact list of ten critical fiber trunks and three satellite relays that form the minimum cut, tactical teams are immediately dispatched with literal bolt cutters and explosive charges to the physical server farms. The mathematical proof of the minimum cut is their only assurance that this drastic, self-inflicted amputation will successfully save the heart of the nation.
