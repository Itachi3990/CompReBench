The Interplanetary Mining Consortium is preparing to launch the 'Surveyor 9', a deep-space autonomous probe tasked with collecting physical core samples from fifty distinct asteroid mining sites scattered across the Kuiper Belt. During the mission planning phase, the astrophysics department became bogged down in irrelevant theoretical models. Half of the department insisted on calculating a "minimum spanning tree of the asteroids," arguing that they should map out the absolute minimum total distance required to connect all fifty sites with theoretical communication lasers, completely ignoring the fact that a probe cannot travel in a branched tree structure. 

The other half of the department was obsessed with calculating a "topological ordering of the flybys." They spent months analyzing the asteroid mass in gigatons and the precise chronological dates of their orbital alignments, trying to establish a strict, mandated chronological sequence for the visits based on gravitational windows. They assumed that organizing the itinerary chronologically by orbital mechanics was the most critical factor for the mission's success. 

However, the lead mission director immediately scrapped both the communication tree and the chronological sequence. While it might seem like the primary objective is to find the absolute shortest path from Earth to the furthest, most valuable rock, or to establish a strict chronological timeline for the flybys based on orbits, these metrics ignore the severe operational limits of the Surveyor 9. 

The probe is a single physical entity with a strictly limited fuel capacity. It cannot branch out to visit multiple sites simultaneously, and it is not simply traveling to one specific destination. The probe must physically travel between the asteroids, and each specific jump from one rock to another requires a precise expenditure of thruster fuel, heavily dependent on localized gravity wells and the relative velocities of the two bodies. 

Therefore, the mission director is NOT trying to connect the asteroids with a minimum spanning tree. They are NOT trying to establish a topological chronological sequence, and they are NOT trying to find the shortest route to a single target. 

Instead, the director needs a singular, perfect flight plan. The algorithm must analyze the entire map of the fifty mining sites and the specific fuel costs associated with traveling between any two points in the belt. The objective is to calculate one continuous, unbroken trajectory. This specific flight path must begin at the Earth spacedock, visit every single one of the fifty asteroid mining sites exactly once to collect a sample, and then return the probe safely to the Earth spacedock. The algorithm must evaluate all possible permutations of this massive circuit to identify the one specific sequence of visits that guarantees the total, cumulative expenditure of thruster fuel for the entire round trip is the absolute mathematical minimum.
