In the final, grueling month of the 2028 Presidential Election, the incumbent campaign team is finalizing a massive, cross-country whistle-stop tour for the President. The stakes are incredibly high, and the logistics are a nightmare. Initially, the data analytics department tried to dictate the schedule using a "perfect bipartite matching of demographics." They spent weeks trying to perfectly pair the President's thirty different policy speeches with thirty specific, highly targeted voter demographics across the country, obsessing over the precise number of registered voters in each county and the exact dollar amounts required for localized ad buys. They assumed that mathematically aligning the exact right speech with the exact right demographic was the key to the tour.

Simultaneously, the media strategists were demanding a "shortest path to the electoral college" model. They wanted to calculate the absolute most direct, fastest route from the campaign headquarters in Chicago straight through to the three swing states that would theoretically push the President over the 270-vote threshold. They treated the campaign like a sprint, focused entirely on reaching a single, numerical victory target as quickly as possible.

However, the seasoned Campaign Manager threw both of these reports in the trash. While it might seem like perfectly matching speeches to specific crowds, or finding the absolute fastest path to secure 270 electoral votes is the primary objective of a campaign tour, these strategies completely ignore the physical reality of the candidate and the press corps. The President cannot simply teleport to three swing states and declare victory, nor can he deliver thirty different speeches simultaneously to disconnected demographics. 

The reality is a grueling physical marathon. The campaign has identified exactly forty critical battleground cities that the President absolutely must visit to project strength and unity on national television. The travel time between these cities varies wildly depending on airport access, airspace restrictions, and motorcade logistics. 

Therefore, the Campaign Manager is NOT trying to perfectly pair demographic data. They are NOT trying to find the shortest path to a single electoral victory state, and they are NOT trying to sequence the speeches by importance. 

Instead, they need a singular, master itinerary for 'Air Force One'. The planning algorithm must analyze the travel times between all forty critical battleground cities. The objective is to calculate one continuous, unbroken travel circuit. This specific itinerary must begin at the Chicago headquarters, visit every single one of the forty battleground cities exactly once to host a rally, and then return the President safely back to Chicago for the final election night watch party. The algorithm must evaluate all possible permutations of this massive tour to identify the one specific sequence of visits that guarantees the total, cumulative travel time for the entire round trip is the absolute mathematical minimum, keeping the candidate as rested as physically possible.
